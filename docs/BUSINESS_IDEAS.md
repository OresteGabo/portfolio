# Struct Capital: business ideas to revisit

Saved: 30 October 2026  
Based on our earlier business discussion. These are ideas to validate, not committed features or revenue forecasts. Built by GABO.

## Core opportunity

Help people and organizations turn investment paperwork into reliable records and useful reports.

Struct already has much of this workflow: select a PDF, identify the transaction, review its details, organize the original document, track expected payments and generate reports. The business value to test is less manual work, fewer missing records and clearer reporting.

## Potential customers and revenue models

| Customer | Problem to investigate | Possible revenue model |
|---|---|---|
| Individual investors, including diaspora | Keeping investments across brokers organized | Annual Pro subscription |
| Investment clubs | Shared records, member reporting and contribution tracking | Monthly team subscription |
| Broker firms | Collecting client documents, resolving missing confirmations and producing reports | Business subscription or paid pilot |

These are customer hypotheses. Willingness to pay still needs testing through actual conversations and offers.

## Preferred starting point: investment clubs

A club treasurer has a concrete reporting and reconciliation job. Start by understanding that job before building a team product.

Possible team features to explore:

- A shared portfolio with administrator and read-only member access.
- Contribution and ownership records.
- Expected payments, manually confirmed receipts and statement-verified payments shown separately.
- Monthly member reports.
- An audit trail showing who changed a record and when.

Keep payment states precise: automatically marking a coupon on its due date should have a different label from verifying that money arrived through a statement.

## Individual Pro ideas

Possible paid features:

- Advanced reconciliation between statements, confirmations and recorded investments.
- Multiple portfolios.
- Richer reports.
- Cross-device import history.

Keep original documents accessible and portable. Trust and easy access to a person's own records should remain central to the product.

## Broker business offering

Explore a focused pilot around document collection, missing confirmations and reporting. Ask a broker to demonstrate the current workflow and identify repetitive work before proposing a large integration.

Potential commercial formats are a paid pilot followed by a business subscription. Pricing, permissions, responsibilities and data access would need agreement with the participating firm.

## Validate before building team features

1. Interview five investors and three investment-club treasurers about how they manage records today.
2. Ask them to demonstrate a recent reporting or reconciliation task.
3. Test Struct with their permission and measure time saved, parsing corrections and missing records found.
4. Offer a paid pilot to people who found measurable value.
5. Use repeated usage and payment decisions to choose the next features.

Useful questions:

- Which task takes the most time each month?
- Where are the original documents kept?
- How are errors and missing confirmations discovered?
- Who prepares reports, and who reads or approves them?
- What would make Struct worth paying for?

## Illustrative revenue target

10 clubs × RWF 30,000 per month = **RWF 300,000 monthly revenue**, before expenses.

This is an example to test, not a forecast or a validated price. Account for hosting, storage, parsing, support, payment collection and maintenance before treating revenue as profit.

## Next business milestone

**Three paying customers who repeatedly use the document-to-report workflow.**

Their experience should guide development priorities. Avoid building every proposed business feature before learning whether customers return and pay.
